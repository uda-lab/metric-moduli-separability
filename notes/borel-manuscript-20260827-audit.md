### 2.3 `lem:one-point-amalgamation` — PASS + EXTERNAL (§4, standard)

**Statement.** For sets `X₀, X₁` meeting in exactly one point `z`, with metrics
`d₀, d₁`, the amalgam routing every cross pair through `z` (`d₀(x,z) + d₁(z,y)`
in the metric case, `d₀(x,z) ∨ d₁(z,y)` in the ultrametric case) is a metric,
resp. an ultrametric; it is complete when both pieces are.

**Check.** The metric and ultrametric assertions are cited (§4). The
completeness half is proved in the manuscript, and the argument checks out. A
Cauchy sequence is either eventually inside one piece — where it converges by
completeness of that piece, and its limit is a limit in the amalgam because
each `d_i` is the restriction of `d` — or else meets both `X_i ∖ {z}`
infinitely often, in which case for `n ≥ N` and `x_n ≠ z` one may pick `m ≥ N`
with `x_m` in the *other* piece, and the amalgamation formula gives
`d(x_n,z) ≤ d(x_n,x_m) < ε`; the same estimate is trivial when `x_n = z`, so
`x_n → z`. The dichotomy is exhaustive because `z` lies in both pieces: a
sequence with only finitely many terms in `X₁ ∖ {z}` is eventually in `X₀`. ✓

**Significance.** This lemma is what makes the v1 §2.11 repair of
`thm:complete-metric-nonborel` a one-line consequence rather than a bespoke
argument, and it is reused at `thm:complete-ultrametric-nonborel` and
`cor:complete-ultrametric-borel-separability`. Note that its completeness
clause needs **no** uniform gap at the glue point, unlike the hypothesis form
the Lean development uses (`isCompleteMetricOn_amalgam`, v1 note §7.5).

### 2.4 `lem:maximal-separated-set` — PASS

**Statement.** (metric clause) Every nonseparable metric space contains a
closed discrete subspace of cardinality `ℵ₁`; more precisely there is an
`ε > 0` admitting no countable `ε`-net, and every maximal `ε`-separated subset
is an uncountable closed discrete `ε`-net. (ultrametric clause) For an
ultrametric space, `a > 0` and a maximal `a`-separated `A`, the open
`a`-balls centred at `A` form a clopen partition, and the centre map
`π : X → A` is a continuous retraction with `d(x,π(x)) < a`.

**Check.** The metric clause is the standard separated-set argument the v1
note §2.8 re-derived inline, now stated once with the `ε`-net clause made
explicit — which is what `cor:nonseparable-metric-nonborel` and
`cor:nonseparable-ultrametric-nonborel` consume. The ultrametric clause: two
balls of equal radius in an ultrametric space are disjoint or equal, and
equality would place distinct centres at distance `< a`; balls are clopen, so
`π` is continuous, being constant on each block. ✓

**Note for the Lean layer.** `IshikiExt.exists_aleph1_closed_discrete_separated`
formalises only the "closed discrete of cardinality `ℵ₁`" conclusion and drops
the `ε`-net clause. The `ε`-net clause is load-bearing in
`cor:nonseparable-metric-nonborel` (it is what supplies `a_x` in
`eq:metric-extensor-component-bound`), so a sibling lemma exposing it is
needed there; this is the ε-net item of PR #119 (Issue #107), formalised as
`exists_aleph1_closed_discrete_separated_net` in `Ext/GeneralSpace.lean`.

### 2.7 `lem:metric-extensor-pullbacks` — PASS + EXTERNAL (E1)

**Statement.** For `Ẽ` supplied by E1 and `E = Ẽ|_{Met(A)}`:
`Ẽ⁻¹(Met(X)) = Met(A)`; `Ẽ⁻¹(BMet(X)) = BMet(A)`;
`Ẽ⁻¹(𝔠(E(d))) = 𝔠(d)` for every `d ∈ Met(A)`; and, when `X` is completely
metrizable and the extensor is chosen per E1's completeness clause,
`Ẽ⁻¹(Comp(X)) = Comp(A)`.

**Check.** The compatibility identity is E1's own
`Ẽ(Met(A)) = Ẽ(CPM(A)) ∩ Met(X)` read as a preimage, using injectivity of the
isometric `Ẽ`. Boundedness: `⊆` is E1's clause on `BMet`; `⊇` because a
member of `Ẽ⁻¹(BMet(X))` is already in `Met(A)` and is the restriction of a
bounded metric to `A²`. Component: `Ẽ` is an isometric embedding on all of
`CPM(A)`, so `𝒟_X(Ẽ(p),E(d)) = 𝒟_A(p,d)`, and membership in the component
adds only compatibility, already handled. Completeness: `⊆` needs `A` closed
in `X` and `Ẽ(p)|_{A²} = p`, so `p` is the restriction of a complete metric to
a closed subspace; `⊇` is E1's completeness clause. ✓

**Significance.** This lemma is the manuscript's own version of three separate
repairs and derivations the v1 audit had to record by hand — the `BMet`
pullback identified as the missing link of v1 §2.10, the component pullback
now needed by `cor:nonseparable-metric-nonborel`, and the `Comp` identity of
v1 §2.12. All three are now printed.

### 2.11 `prop:dense-aleph1-nonborel-cantor` — PASS + EXTERNAL (E4c)

**Statement (strengthened).** There is `S ⊆ 2^ω` with `Card(S) = ℵ₁`, with
**both** `S` and `2^ω ∖ S` dense, and with `S` non-Borel in `2^ω`.

**v1 verdict was FIX.** v1's proof was "follows from the fact that `2^ω` is
Borel isomorphic to `[0,1]`", and a Borel isomorphism does not transport
density.

**Check of the received proof.** Take disjoint countable dense `P, Q ⊆ 2^ω`.
`2^ω ∖ P` is an uncountable standard Borel space, so a Borel isomorphism
`f : [0,1] → 2^ω ∖ P` exists (cited, **E4c**). With `S₀` from
`prop:dense-aleph1-nonborel-interval`, put `S = f(S₀) ∪ Q`. Density comes from

**Note that `f(S₀)` and `Q` need not be disjoint**: `Q` avoids `P` but lies in
`2^ω ∖ P`, which is exactly the range of `f`, so `S ∖ Q` is `f(S₀) ∖ Q` rather
than `f(S₀)`. The manuscript's one-line "every countable set is Borel" is the
correct hint for this step, but the step is the countable correction just
given, not a set equality.
✓ **The Borel isomorphism is used only for Borel structure and cardinality,
never to transport density**, which is precisely the v1 defect; and the

Kechris.

### 2.16 `cor:bounded-metric-complete-sigma-compact` — PASS + EXTERNAL (E3, E5, E6)

**Statement (strengthened from two-way to five-way).** For metrizable `X` the
following are equivalent: `Met(X)` completely metrizable; `BMet(X)` completely
metrizable; every component of `Met(X)` completely metrizable; some component
completely metrizable; `X` σ-compact.

**v1 verdict was FIX**: v1 derived the two-way form by "combining" Koshino
with the `Met` corollary, and the `⇒` direction needed a `BMet`-Borel
separability statement that v1 did not have.

**The cycle.** `met ⇒ each-component ⇒ met`, `each-component ⇒ bmet ⇒
sigma-compact ⇒ each-component`, and `each-component ⇒ some-component ⇒
sigma-compact`. This does connect all five. ✓

**Elementary implications.** Components are clopen, so a closed subspace of a
completely metrizable space is completely metrizable (`met ⇒ each-component`);
`Met(X)` is the topological sum of its clopen components and a topological sum
of completely metrizable spaces is completely metrizable (`each-component ⇒
met`); the bounded compatible metrics form exactly one component, nonempty
because `min{d,1}` is compatible (`each-component ⇒ bmet`). ✓

**`bmet ⇒ sigma-compact`.** `BMet(X)` completely metrizable makes it `G_δ`,
hence Borel, in `CPM(X)`; `thm:borel-separability` — in its new `BMet` form —
gives separability; Koshino then gives σ-compactness. ✓ **This is the v1
§2.10 repair, discharged by the restatement of `thm:borel-separability`.**

**New direction A, `sigma-compact ⇒ each-component`.** Checked in full.
`P_d = {p ∈ CPM(X) : 𝒟_X(p,d) < ∞}` is `𝒟_X`-complete: a `𝒟_X`-Cauchy
sequence is Cauchy for the truncated metric, converges in `CPM(X)` by
`lem:cpm-complete`, and the limit stays at finite distance from `d`. With
`A_n = B(o,2^n;d)` closed and σ-compact hence separable, Koshino makes
`BMet(A_n)` completely metrizable and `thm:complete-metrizability-g-delta`
makes it `G_δ` in `BCPM(A_n)`. The restriction maps
`r_n : P_d → BCPM(A_n)` are 1-Lipschitz, hence continuous; they are
well-defined because `d` is bounded on `A_n²` and `p` is within `𝒟_X(p,d)` of
it. The characterization `eq:component-restriction-characterization`,
`𝔠 = ⋂_n r_n⁻¹(BMet(A_n))` inside `P_d`, holds:
`⊆` because restricting a compatible bounded-on-`A_n²` metric gives a member
of `BMet(A_n)`;
`⊇` because every distinct pair lies in some `A_n`, making `p` definite, and
because for `x ∈ X`, a neighbourhood `U` of `x` and `N` with
`2^N > d(o,x) + K + 1` (where `K = 𝒟_X(p,d)`) one gets
`d(x,y) > K + 1` and so `p(x,y) > 1` for every `y ∉ A_N`, confining any
`p`-ball of radius `< 1` around `x` to `A_N`, where `p|_{A_N²}` is already
compatible — while continuity of `p` makes every `p`-ball open in the original
topology, so the two topologies agree. Hence `𝔠` is `G_δ` in the completely
metrizable `P_d`, and the reverse direction of
`thm:complete-metrizability-g-delta` applies. ✓

**New direction B, `some-component ⇒ sigma-compact`.** Checked in full. For
`𝔠` completely metrizable, `d ∈ 𝔠`, and `A_n` as above, the restriction
`R_n : 𝔠 → BMet(A_n)` is 1-Lipschitz and well-defined. **E5** (Ishiki's
interpolation theorem) supplies, for `m ∈ 𝔠` and `e ∈ BMet(A_n)`, an
`M ∈ Met(X)` with `M|_{A_n²} = e` and the exact distance equality
`𝒟_X(M,m) = 𝒟_{A_n}(e, m|_{A_n²})` (`eq:metric-interpolation-distance`). This
gives surjectivity (the distance is finite, so `M ∈ 𝔠`) and the ball-image
identity `eq:restriction-map-ball-image`, whose `⊆` half is
1-Lipschitzness and whose `⊇` half is the interpolation with the exact
distance; so `R_n` is open. **E6** (Hausdorff's theorem that a metrizable
image of a complete metric space under a continuous open map is completely
metrizable) then makes `BMet(A_n)` completely metrizable, whence — via `G_δ`,
Borel, `thm:borel-separability` and Koshino applied to `A_n` — each `A_n` is
σ-compact and so is `X = ⋃_n A_n`. ✓

**Implicit step recorded.** In direction B the passage from "`BMet(A_n)`
completely metrizable" to `thm:borel-separability` compresses the `G_δ` ⇒
Borel step that the `bmet ⇒ sigma-compact` branch spells out. Immediate, but
worth stating in a revision for symmetry.

**Verdict: PASS + EXTERNAL.** The Lean statement gate for this form is #108.

binding:

1. **The `|S| = ℵ₁` "not coanalytic" claim is not a ZFC theorem.** The ¬CH
   branch is refutable: there are models of ZFC + ¬CH — any ccc forcing
   extension of `L` keeping `ω₁^L = ω₁`, e.g. adding `ℵ₂` Cohen reals — in
   which the largest thin `Π¹₁` set `C₁` is a **coanalytic** dense set of
   cardinality `ℵ₁`, a legitimate output of the old recipe. By
   Mansfield–Solovay, a ZFC repair would need to produce `S` with `S ⊄ L[a]`
   for every real `a`, which the audit could not obtain in ZFC and which fails
   for the recipe as stated. Only the asymmetric half ("not analytic", by
   cardinality `ℵ₁ < 𝔠` plus Souslin's perfect set theorem) survived in ZFC
   under ¬CH.
2. **The classical analytic/coanalytic pointclasses are the wrong notion in
   the nonseparable ambient**: `Σ¹₁` sets are separable by definition, and both
   `BMet(D)` and its complement in `BCPM(D)` have density `2^{ℵ₁}`, so the old
   theorem was vacuously true under the classical reading and needed a bespoke
   Souslin-`F` convention to mean anything.

**The Borel statement replaces both**, in v1 and in the received source alike.
The perfect set theorem for *Borel* sets is a ZFC theorem, making
`prop:dense-aleph1-nonborel-interval` a clean unconditional dichotomy, and the
Borel σ-algebra of the nonseparable ambient is generated by its own topology
with all needed closure properties — no Polish ambient, no Souslin operation,
no pointclass convention. **No analytic or coanalytic statement appears
anywhere in the received source**, and none may be reintroduced into the
verification targets. Any future strengthening toward projective classes must
reopen the analysis above as a research question, not as a verification
target.

Correspondingly: `note_by_ishiki_1.pdf`, `note_by_ishiki_2.pdf` and the
standalone older `main.tex` are that superseded draft's artifacts. None of
them is the latest source, and no document in this repository may describe
them as one.

## 8. Record of Issue #113 — `cor:metric-space-complete-separability`

**Verdict: FIX in the 2026-08-27 received source; PASS in v1.** These must not
be averaged.

**The defect.** The printed proof takes `Met(X)` completely metrizable,
derives that it is `G_δ` and hence Borel in `CPM(X)` by
`thm:complete-metrizability-g-delta`, and then concludes "by
`thm:borel-separability`, `X` is separable". But in the received source
`thm:borel-separability`'s hypothesis is Borelness of **`BMet(X)`**, not of
`Met(X)`, so that step does not follow from the theorem as stated. Verified
directly in the source.

**Why it is a regression, not a long-standing error.** In arXiv:2608.19618 v1
`thm:borel-separability` is stated for `Met(X)`, and this corollary — which v1
printed without a proof — appeals to it soundly. The defect appeared when the
draft restated the theorem for `BMet(X)` without updating the corollary. The
v1 verdict on this corollary therefore stands (v1 note §2.9, PASS + EXTERNAL);
only the received source's version is defective.

**The statement is true, and two repairs are available.**

1. *Preferred.* `cor:nonseparable-metric-nonborel`(2) immediately precedes it
   and gives, for nonseparable `X`, that `Met(X)` is non-Borel in `CPM(X)`.
   Complete metrizability of `Met(X)` gives Borelness, so the contrapositive
   gives separability directly. One line, and it uses the draft's own new
   theorem package.
2. *Alternative.* The bounded compatible metrics form a single component of
   `Met(X)`, which the draft itself uses in
   `cor:bounded-metric-complete-sigma-compact`. Components are clopen in
   `Met(X)`, so a Borel `Met(X)` makes `BMet(X)` Borel in `CPM(X)` by
   `lem:relative-borel-inheritance`, and `thm:borel-separability` then applies.

**The nomenclature half.** The abstract and the introduction both announce
"`Met(X)` Borel implies `X` separable", and the introduction cites
`thm:borel-separability` for it. That claim is true — it is the contrapositive
of `cor:nonseparable-metric-nonborel`(2) — but it is not what the cited
theorem states. Either restate `thm:borel-separability` for `Met(X)` as v1
did, or give the contrapositive a labelled home of its own. This is the same
class of defect as the second introduction cross-reference recorded in §2.21,
which concerns the `Met(Z)` claim.

**Lean impact: none.** `Ext/CompleteMetrizability.lean` proves "`Met(X)`
completely metrizable implies `X` separable"
(`separable_of_isCompletelyMetrizableSpace_CPM_MetOn_of_isometricExtensorOn`)
through the verified `Met`-transfer theorem, **not** through the invalid direct
application of the `BMet` theorem. The Lean route therefore already matches
the preferred repair rather than the draft's proof text, and no Lean change is
required by #113. Recording that correspondence, rather than leaving it to
look like a discrepancy, is what #113's repository-side acceptance criteria
ask for.

**Disposition.** The two manuscript-edit criteria of #113 are author-side: the
manuscript is not in this repository and will not be imported. The two
repository-side criteria are discharged by this note — the theorem-by-theorem
record above carries the FIX/PASS split, and the Lean correspondence is stated
in the preceding paragraph.
