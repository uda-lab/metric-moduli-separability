\begin{abstract}
  We investigate Conjecture~5.1 of Ishiki~\cite{Ishiki2021}, which
  concerns the complete metrizability of the space $\Met(D)$ of metrics
  on a discrete space $D$, equipped with the supremum-metric topology.
  We present and analyse a proof candidate showing that $\Met(D)$ is
  \emph{not} completely metrizable when $\lvert D\rvert = \aleph_1$.
  The argument has not yet passed independent mathematical audit;
  this paper should be regarded as a working draft.
\end{abstract}

% ------------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Let $D$ be an uncountable discrete topological space, and let
$\Met(D)$ denote the set of all metrics on $D$.  We equip $\Met(D)$
with the topology induced by the supremum distance
\[
  \rho(d, d') \;=\; \sup_{x,y\in D} \lvert d(x,y) - d'(x,y)\rvert.
\]
Ishiki~\cite{Ishiki2021} raised a series of conjectures about the
topological structure of $\Met(D)$.  Conjecture~5.1 asserts that,
for $\lvert D\rvert = \aleph_1$, the space $\Met(D)$ is not
completely metrizable.

  A subspace of a completely metrizable space is completely metrizable
  if and only if it is a $\Gdelta$ set.
\end{theorem}
